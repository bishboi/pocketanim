# Combustion

Why does a gas flame give out heat? Burning is a chemical reaction between a fuel and oxygen.

Methane is the main part of natural gas. When methane burns, each molecule reacts with two molecules of oxygen.

The equation is CH4 + 2O2 -> CO2 + 2H2O. The products are carbon dioxide and water.

Breaking the bonds in methane and oxygen takes energy, but forming the bonds in carbon dioxide and water releases more. The difference is given out as heat.

If there is too little oxygen, carbon monoxide forms instead, and carbon monoxide is poisonous.

Ethanol burns in the same way: C2H5OH + 3O2 -> 2CO2 + 3H2O. It is used as a fuel in cars in Brazil.

Every combustion of a hydrocarbon adds carbon dioxide to the air, which is why burning fuels warms the climate.
