# The Battle of Plassey, 1757

In the middle of the eighteenth century Bengal was the richest province of Mughal India. Its trade in cotton cloth, silk, saltpetre and opium drew the English East India Company, the French and the Dutch to trading posts along the Hooghly river. The Nawab of Bengal ruled from Murshidabad, and the Company's main settlement was Calcutta.

In 1756 a young Nawab, Siraj ud-Daulah, came to power. He was angered that the Company had strengthened the defences of Calcutta without his leave. In June 1756 his army attacked and captured Calcutta.

The Company sent Robert Clive from Madras with troops and a fleet. Clive retook Calcutta in January 1757. In March 1757 he attacked and captured the French settlement of Chandernagore.

Clive now joined a conspiracy against the Nawab. Mir Jafar, the Nawab's own commander, secretly agreed to betray him in return for the throne. The banker Jagat Seth and the merchant Omichund were part of the plot, and the alliance was sealed with promises of money.

On 13 June 1757 Clive marched north from Chandernagore. His army crossed the Bhagirathi river at Katwa on 22 June and camped in a mango grove near the village of Plassey that night.

Clive had about 3,000 men, of whom about 800 were Europeans, and 10 guns. The Nawab's army had about 50,000 men and 53 guns, with a small party of French gunners.

At 8 a.m. on 23 June 1757 the Nawab's guns opened fire on the mango grove. The French guns fired from near the small tank, and the Company's troops sheltered behind the mud bank of the grove.

Around noon heavy rain fell on the field. The Company covered its powder with tarpaulins, but the Nawab's powder was soaked and his guns fell silent.

Mir Madan, the Nawab's most loyal commander, charged the grove with his cavalry, expecting the English guns to be wet. The English guns fired, and Mir Madan was killed.

Mir Jafar held back and did not attack. At 2 p.m. the Nawab's army began to retreat to its camp. The Company advanced and stormed the French position, and by 5 p.m. the Nawab fled towards Murshidabad.

After the battle Mir Jafar was made Nawab of Bengal. The Company won the Battle of Buxar in 1764, and in 1765 it received the Diwani of Bengal, the right to collect its taxes. Plassey was the first step towards British rule in India.
