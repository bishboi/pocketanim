In 1853, four American warships sailed into Tokyo Bay.
Japan had kept itself closed to the world for two hundred years, and now it saw how far behind it was.
So its new leaders made a bet: learn from the West, and do it fast.
They sent students abroad, hired foreign engineers, and laid railways across the islands.
Factories rose where rice fields had been.
And in 1905, just fifty years later, Japan shocked the world by defeating Russia.
